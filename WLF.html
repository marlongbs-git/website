WLF Hydroponic Labs

Automated indoor growing system for community food distribution

WLF Hydroponics builds custom growing systems for community food distribution. On a three-person team, I helped design and build the first iteration of an automated indoor farm, from an empty room to a working system.

I owned the room model, the piping layout, the seismic safety bracketing, and the structural frame, and shared fabrication and sourcing with the team.

  • Role Mechanical design and fabrication
  • Team 3 people, WLF Hydroponics
  • Tools SolidWorks (piping, sheet metal, McMaster-Carr add-ins), 3D printing, metal shop

Highlights

  • Built a hydroponic piping network with 10+ valves and pumps, with safety controls to prevent leaks and over-pressure failures
  • Fabricated an 11-gauge sheet metal bracket without a press brake that met SFU Faculty of Applied Science seismic and anti-vibration requirements
  • Built the aluminum extrusion frame and gutter assembly that forms the main structure of the grow system
  • Designed a 3D-printed electronics enclosure with PCB mounts and cable routing to protect against humidity
  • Modeled and documented a dosing system for future implementation

Design Process

1. Starting from a blank room

The team began with rough sketches and no set dimensions, materials, or build plan. I modeled the designated room in our engineering building to map the usable space, including the plant nursery, reservoir containers, and emergency drain access.

I then laid out the piping with SolidWorks' built-in libraries for connectors, elbows, and pipe runs, and produced drawings for construction. With a limited budget and a sustainability mandate, every part went through a decision matrix and a review with the finance team, with local suppliers preferred.

2. Prototyping and fabrication

Much of the hardware was prototyped with 3D printing, which meant modeling every sensor and electronic part so it would fit its enclosure.

The hardest part was physical. An 11-gauge sheet metal bracket had to be bent without a press brake. Working with the machinist over several hours, we produced a bracket that met SFU's seismic safety requirements.

With only three people, each of us owned large parts of the system. That pushed me to solve problems hands-on in ways a larger team often doesn't.

Skills Used

Mechanical Design

Piping network design in SolidWorks using the piping library, sheet metal tools, and the McMaster-Carr add-in for fasteners.

Rapid Prototyping

Iterative modeling and 3D printing of sensor mounts, enclosures, and the dosing system.

Metal Fabrication

Cutting, filing, sheet metal forming, and drill pressing alongside the machinist.
