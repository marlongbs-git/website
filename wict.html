Women in Clean Tech: ASHRAE Building Design Competition

Lighting design for a net-zero public library in São Paulo

The ASHRAE Setty Family Foundation Net Zero Energy Design Competition asked teams to design an energy-efficient public library in São Paulo, Brazil, minimizing life-cycle cost and carbon footprint while keeping occupants comfortable.

As the team's electrical designer, I owned the lighting system: load calculations, fixture selection and modeling, and control automation, all to ASHRAE standards.

  • Role Electrical designer (lighting)
  • Team Women in Clean Tech, SFU
  • When January 2025 - May 2025
  • Tools DIALux evo, Excel, ASHRAE 90.1-2022 and 189.1

Highlights

  • Designed Level 2 lighting at 3,165 W against a 10,720 W code maximum. Scaled across the building, that's about 10,845 W, roughly 80% below the 54,158 W allowed
  • Built Excel tools that calculate lighting power density for every room from its dimensions and ceiling height
  • Designed controls with occupancy sensing, daylight dimming, and scheduled shutoff
  • Selected LED fixtures with decision matrices weighing efficiency, cost, life cycle, and installation flexibility

Design Process

1. Setting the code limit

Using ASHRAE 90.1-2022's Space-by-Space Method, I categorized every room to find its maximum allowed lighting power density and control requirements. That set a code maximum of 10,720 W for Level 2 and 54,158 W for the whole building.

I built Excel tools to automate the per-room calculations from room dimensions and ceiling height, so layout changes updated the totals right away.

2. Fixtures, modeling, and controls

I chose surface-mounted LED fixtures for efficiency and lifespan, and modeled all of Level 2 in DIALux evo with manufacturer fixture data and real furniture layouts. Controls combine daylight dimming, occupancy sensing, and scheduled shutoff.

Result

Level 2 came in at 3,165 W, under a third of its code maximum. Scaled to the whole building, the estimate is about 10,845 W, roughly 80% below the limit, before counting extra savings from daylight dimming along the curtain walls. Outdoor fixtures are specified for low backlight, uplight, and glare ratings.

Skills Used
